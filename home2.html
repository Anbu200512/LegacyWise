People first

Make space for the people who matter most

A thoughtful plan starts with the people you want to care for. Consider the needs of children, partners, and other loved ones, and how your wishes can be made clear for them.

  • Think through the needs of dependents and family
  • Choose people you trust for important roles
  • Record wishes in a clear, considered way
A little clarity goes a long way

Why make a plan ahead of time?

Planning gives you a chance to think through important decisions calmly and explain them clearly to the people who may need to act on your wishes.

Make your wishes known

Clear documents can help others understand the choices you have made.

Prepare the people you trust

Thinking about key roles in advance helps you choose people carefully.

Reduce avoidable uncertainty

Organized information gives loved ones a clearer place to start.

Home & property

Include your home in the bigger picture

Your home may be one of the most significant parts of your estate. Understanding ownership and how property fits with your other wishes can help make your overall plan easier to follow.

Explore estate planning guidance
Before you begin

Gather a clear picture of ownership

Property can be owned in different ways, and ownership details may affect how it fits into an estate plan. Collect relevant records and raise questions about shared ownership with an appropriately qualified professional.

Helpful to note: whose name is on the title, whether ownership is shared, and where related records are kept.

Your financial picture

Bring your assets and beneficiaries into view

A clear inventory can help you see how your accounts, property, and other assets fit together. Reviewing beneficiary designations alongside your documents can help keep your intentions aligned.

  • Gather an overview of assets and accounts
  • Review named beneficiaries when circumstances change
  • Keep key information organized and current
A practical first step

Start with a few simple questions

You do not need every answer before you begin. These prompts can help organize your thoughts for a conversation with a qualified professional.

What matters most?

List the people, values, and priorities you want your plan to reflect.

Who do you trust?

Consider who could take on important responsibilities if needed.

What needs a closer look?

Note questions about ownership, beneficiaries, or existing documents.

Put wishes in writing

Turn good intentions into clear documents

Written documents help communicate decisions and provide direction to the people responsible for carrying them out. Work with appropriately qualified professionals to make sure documents reflect your wishes and meet local requirements.

Learn about will drafting

Clear records make a plan easier to follow

Keep important documents organized, let the appropriate people know where they can be found, and review them with qualified professionals when your circumstances or local requirements change.

Learn about document review
Keep it current

Revisit your plan as life changes

A plan is not a one-time task. Major changes—such as a move, a new child, a marriage, or a change in assets—can be a good prompt to review your documents and named decision-makers.
